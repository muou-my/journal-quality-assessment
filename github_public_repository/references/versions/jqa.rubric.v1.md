# REF-inspired scoring rubric

Version: jqa.rubric.v1

Version identifier added for traceability; the existing rating anchors are unchanged.

This rubric is an operational adaptation for title-and-abstract assessment. It is
not an official REF scoring rubric and should be frozen before formal evaluation.

For each dimension, assign an integer from 1 to 4.

## Originality
- **1:** Little identifiable novelty in the stated question, approach, evidence, or synthesis.
- **2:** Some incremental novelty, but the contribution appears limited or largely confirmatory.
- **3:** Clear and meaningful originality in the question, approach, evidence, or synthesis.
- **4:** Exceptional originality with a potentially field-shaping contribution.

## Significance
- **1:** Limited apparent contribution to knowledge, understanding, policy, or practice.
- **2:** Relevant contribution, but likely narrow in importance or reach.
- **3:** Important contribution with clear implications for the field or relevant practice.
- **4:** Exceptional significance with substantial potential implications beyond a narrow topic.

## Rigour
- **1:** Abstract indicates substantial concerns about conceptual or methodological soundness.
- **2:** Some credible elements, but important aspects of design, evidence, or analysis are unclear.
- **3:** Coherent and appropriate approach with evidence of sound design and analysis.
- **4:** Exceptionally robust and well-justified approach, with strong alignment between question,
  methods, evidence, analysis, and conclusions.

## Evidence and uncertainty rules

- Judge only what is stated in the supplied title and abstract.
- Do not assume methods, sample sizes, robustness checks, or findings not reported.
- A short abstract may justify `high` uncertainty even when a provisional score is given.
- Evidence notes must point to information actually present in the supplied text.
- Do not use journal name, author identity, affiliation, citations, or journal metrics.
